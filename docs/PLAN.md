# One-room live viewer

The page is a quiet, live window into one recorded room. The residents' and places' own pixel drawings provide its character. It reads public city facts anonymously and never writes anything back. The eight points below describe the finished page; the four PRs below name what is delivered at each step.

## The eight-point shape

1. **One room fills the view.** The place's 8×8 drawing tiles the floor with only a light dim. An undrawn place has one warm plain floor. Resident drawings stand about 48–64 screen pixels tall; things are smaller. Names stay at one readable screen size beneath figures.
2. **The viewer chooses a resident or a place.** Following a resident changes rooms only after that resident has walked through the source door and fully left the room. Choosing a place keeps that room on screen as residents come and go. The initial room has the most recorded activity in the thirty minutes ending at the newest recorded timestamp within thirty minutes of the next newest timestamp, counting a move at its destination. An isolated future-dated record cannot move a clustered window or enter its score. When no two records are within thirty minutes, the newest record anchors the window; a single available record still counts. The browser's clock cannot change that choice. Headcount breaks ties or chooses when that window is empty. Places with public child places are skipped while a public room without public children is available. The page never changes rooms on its own.
3. **The page is live only.** It seeds from the current public state and follows the public change feed. A recorded departure moves from its source position through the door and fully out at one linear pace of 140 CSS pixels per second. A recorded arrival enters at the door and walks to a reachable free target sized for the 56-pixel resident. When only the source is selected and the resident is not followed, the walk finishes once the figure has left through that door; there is no unseen arrival leg to hold delivery. Selecting that destination later shows the resident at a free spot when space allows. Moves entirely outside the view update their recorded destination immediately, without waiting for a displayed doorway. Residents that are not sleeping bob when idle: every awake standing figure gently bobs by two pixels, using the same height as its walk bob and a slower 1.8-second rhythm. Safe idle steps still move 12–24 CSS pixels at 20 CSS pixels per second and become due every 9–11 seconds. Idle motion is decoration, never a city fact.
4. **Speech stays beside its speaker and remains readable.** A warm cream card types the complete recorded note at one fixed screen size. Long bubbles scroll, like the terminal follow view. Each note gets a fixed-height card capped by the room height. As whole lines arrive, the content scrolls upward; the full revealed body remains inside the card and can be scrolled back to. The end holds for a few seconds. There are no pages, clipped bodies, or ellipses. The document never scrolls and the controls stay at the bottom of the viewport. Native browser text stays at 14 pixels with a 20-pixel line height. Short notes keep their normal typing pace; long bodies reveal faster to fit a fifteen-second total lifetime, including the finished-note hold. Queued notes never block live delivery or the live clock, whether they wait for a room turn or their own speaker’s card. A visible card alone does not block either. Queued non-note work and active walks, effects, carry blocks, sparkles, showing notices, blocked attempts, transfers, invention floats and agreement floats still hold delivery. One card appears in a room at a time, and notes across resident queues take that room in recorded change-ID order, including an earlier note waiting behind its resident's move. Pause finishes the current sentence or the current measured whole display line, whichever boundary comes first, without revealing a word fragment, then freezes presentation at that exact boundary. Abbreviation dots, including titles such as Dr. and dotted forms such as a.m. or U.S., do not end a sentence.
5. **The room log wraps and scrolls.** The text in the log is not cut off. It wraps to the available full width and keeps the recent entries for this room, newest at the bottom. Long notes show whole, without ellipses. The strip stays about three lines tall on desktop and two on the phone; it scrolls inside itself without moving the pickers or Pause. New entries follow the bottom unless the reader has scrolled back. The page itself never scrolls. Other-room and quiet-room activity stay out. The room name, names, speech cards, this quiet log, and honest read status remain the only on-screen words.
6. **Controls stay small.** There is one resident picker, one place picker, and Pause. Remove sleeping residents altogether: a resident marked asleep by the public census has no figure, name label, or seat in the room. They appear again when a later record says they are awake. The log still reports recorded sleep and wake changes. A sound control may remain only as one small corner toggle if sound is kept. There is no map, camera control, Focus, replay, rewind, speed control, or UI-hiding control.
7. **Night is soft and warm.** The recorded time may tint the room after dark, but the tint preserves the drawings' colors. Recorded thing-use glow and lit windows remain.
8. **Desktop and phone share the same room.** The canvas uses all available space. Desktop keeps the pickers and Pause in one compact footer row. At 375 pixels wide, the pickers stack at the bottom and the room-name header stays tiny.

## Rules that always hold

- Draw only recorded facts. Figures leave rooms, speech appears, and objects react only when the public record says they did.
- Positions within a room are presentation. PR one introduced standing-band allocation; PR two derives it from the current 56-pixel resident art, with a 60-pixel pitch that leaves room for the bob. Thing art is 32 pixels. The shared allocator reserves each thing's complete 32-pixel footprint and each resident's complete 56-pixel footprint. Things and residents have a cute, pretty label showing their full names: one small rounded cream card with a soft shadow. Long names scroll continuously inside that fixed-width label at a slow pace, briefly pausing at the start of each loop. One text object moves under a mask; its text is not rendered again each frame. A tiny kind mark appears for things when it fits. The labels keep the collision rule, so hidden labels stay hidden. Names use a fixed 13-pixel screen size and device-density text textures with linear filtering, while drawing textures use nearest-neighbour filtering. Text resolution refreshes when the window moves between displays, including the room name. The canvas backing store follows device density, with a compensated camera zoom and unchanged CSS room size, so pixel drawings stay crisp on dense screens. Resident decorations preserve their original glyph scale as they grow with the resident drawing, including activity cues beside the head. Offsets from carried things grow with the thing art. It hides clipped name plates and plates that collide with other plates or figures, while plates that just touch an edge remain eligible. Speakers take seats before idle figures, including a followed idle figure. If several speakers cannot all fit, the room log names the newest unshown speaker and shows the complete recorded note; quiet-room privacy still applies. Other occupants can be hidden when space runs out, so the picture summarises crowded rooms rather than serving as a census. Unchanged frames reuse their placements, and displaced figures return to their preferred spot when it is free. A recorded walk reserves its route and full-size target. When no safe target or path exists, the figure is not drawn for that walk, while its recorded room membership still updates and the quiet activity line describes the move. The viewer never invents an inside-room teleport. Hidden occupants return as room space allows, with speakers taking priority.
- Quiet rooms show their name and no residents, things, speech, or activity. A short status explains why the room is empty, including when following a resident there.
- All city access is anonymous and read-only. The viewer has no key, wallet, credential, or city write path.
- If a city read fails, keep the last successfully read state, let its already recorded walks and bubbles finish, retry politely, and state the read failure honestly in `#live-status`. Clear that status after a successful read. A drawing area too small for the standing band plus one figure has its own status. Recorded walks finish hidden at their destination while it is too small, freeing live delivery; figures are seated again when the view is enlarged. This presentation status never claims the public record failed. With the current margins and figures, the minimum drawing area is 168 by 242 pixels.
- Exact counts belong in the city's other pages. This view never claims a number it did not read.

## Four pull requests

1. **Page and live-room skeleton.** Replace the old page shell with one room, the resident and place pickers, Pause, the room activity line, and honest read status. Seed the picture from current public data and the line from the loaded day's activity. The replay checkpoint is the startup boundary: apply later change IDs once, and skip IDs already delivered. Census pagination and browser time never discard a change. Allocate standing figures and things in the displayed room, resolve name collisions, and use the same positions for activity cues, invention bulbs, handovers, and agreements. Recover from small windows and failed reads without freezing known animation. Remove the old controls from the screen. Floor art, bubble growth, and walking pace remain for the following PRs.
2. **Art and readable words.** Tile place drawings at 32 pixels with only 10% shade. Use one warm plain floor for undrawn rooms. Show 56-pixel residents and 32-pixel things, with fixed 13-pixel names and complete 14-pixel speech, with bounded timing that does not block live delivery. Night uses a warm brown/plum tint capped at 14%, with smooth transitions, lit windows and recorded thing glow. Floor, figure, name, bubble-growth and night-tint policies are plain tested functions.
3. **Recorded motion.** Deliver the 140-CSS-pixel-per-second source-to-door departure and door-to-safe-target arrival paths, safe 12–24-pixel idle steps, recorded room-wide speech queues, and exact-boundary Pause behavior described above. Moves sharing a doorway wait for the earlier walk; a busy doorway does not discard an arrival. At startup, suppress only the first catch-up prefix of applied moves already represented by the paged census destination. Never drop a later move, any note, or any other event, and never use the browser clock to decide what is caught up. Keep motion, allocation, queueing, and timing policies in pure functions with unit tests.
4. **Remove the old viewer.** Delete the files used only by the whole-city map, camera movement, minimap, replay UI, rewind, speeds, director, Focus, and the old whole-city activity log. Keep the owner’s room log. Keep and update the recorded scene fixture and fake-clock test tooling.

The owner-feedback PR after PR three adds idle bobbing, removes sleepers and their seats, lets the full room log wrap and scroll in a fixed strip, changes speech from pages to scrolling cards, and replaces plain name plates with cream labels whose long names scroll. Its real-city capture outputs are `pr-3b-live-desktop.png` and `pr-3b-live-phone.png`.

Each pull request runs `npm run check` and includes real-city screenshots at 1280 pixels and 375 pixels wide. Before each capture, wait for the first change poll and the selected room's outline to resolve (merged or unmergeable after a missing, failed, or hidden-room answer), finish pending picture reads including place drawings, and hold a stable displayed things count for one second. Quiet ancestors use the same visibility rule for drawing and settlement, and a hidden room does not wait for an outline read that cannot start. Every capture sample is bounded by the remaining timeout. New pure behavior gets unit coverage. Browser checks cover only the small user flows and prove that growing a small window redraws figures. No pull request adds a runtime dependency without explaining why, edits `playwright.config.ts` to change browsers, or commits `dist/`.
