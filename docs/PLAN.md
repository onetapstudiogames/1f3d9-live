# One-room live viewer

The page is a quiet, live window into one room. **Live means now.** The residents' and places' own pixel drawings provide its character. It reads public city facts anonymously and never writes anything back. The eight points below describe the finished page; the four PRs below record how it was delivered.

At `/live/`, a startup `?place=<id>` opens a known public room and `?resident=<handle or id>` takes priority to follow an awake resident in a public room, unavailable targets use the usual opening with a plain status, and picker changes replace the current address without navigation while clearing removes both selection parameters.

## The eight-point shape

1. **One room fills the view.** The place's 8×8 drawing tiles the floor behind a soft dim so small objects remain clear. An undrawn place has one warm plain floor. Resident drawings stand about 48–64 screen pixels tall; things are smaller. Names stay at one readable screen size beneath figures.
2. **The viewer chooses a resident or a place.** Following a resident uses that resident's current room as the opening room. It changes rooms only after a newly witnessed move carries that resident through the source door and fully out. Choosing a place keeps that room on screen as residents come and go. Without a followed resident, the initial room is the eligible public leaf with the most awake residents standing there now; ties choose the lowest place ID. Quiet rooms and their descendants are excluded. A public container is skipped when a public child without public children qualifies. No history, timestamp, or browser clock enters this choice. The page never changes rooms on its own.
3. **The page is live only.** It runs at wall-clock time without a playback clock or rate multiplier. Startup reads the change-feed head first and discards its older rows. That cursor is fixed before reading current presence, the place directory, the displayed room outline, and the drawings for the first picture. Changes recorded during those reads remain covered by the first poll. A move already reflected by the current resident destination is logged when witnessed but does not walk twice. There is no replay read, history backfill, history-based chooser, or comparison against replay data. The census and feed refresh every 30 seconds. Only a move first witnessed after that head becomes a walk. A census refresh that finds a resident elsewhere snaps that resident to the current room without inventing travel. A witnessed departure moves from its source position through the door and fully out at one linear pace of 140 CSS pixels per second. A witnessed arrival enters at the door and walks to a reachable free target sized for the 56-pixel resident. When only the source is selected and the resident is not followed, the walk finishes once the figure has left through that door; there is no unseen arrival leg to hold delivery. Selecting that destination later shows the resident at a free spot when space allows. Moves entirely outside the view update their destination immediately, without waiting for a displayed doorway. Residents that are not sleeping bob when idle: every awake standing figure gently bobs by two pixels, using the same height as its walk bob and a slower 1.8-second rhythm. Safe idle steps still move 12–24 CSS pixels at 20 CSS pixels per second and become due every 9–11 seconds. Idle motion is decoration, never a city fact.
4. **Speech stays beside its speaker and remains readable.** A warm cream card types the complete recorded note at one fixed screen size. The card is small: three lines tall and at most 240 pixels wide, and long notes scroll up through it, like the terminal follow view. As whole lines arrive, the content scrolls upward; the full revealed body remains inside the card and can be scrolled back to. The end holds for a few seconds. There are no pages, clipped bodies, or ellipses. The document never scrolls and the controls stay at the bottom of the viewport. Native browser text stays at 14 pixels with a 20-pixel line height. Short notes keep their normal typing pace; long bodies reveal faster to fit a fifteen-second total lifetime, including the finished-note hold. Queued notes never block live delivery or the live clock, whether they wait for a room turn or their own speaker’s card. A visible card alone does not block either. Queued non-note work and active walks, effects, carry blocks, sparkles, showing notices, blocked attempts, transfers, invention floats and agreement floats still hold delivery. One card appears in a room at a time, and notes across resident queues take that room in recorded change-ID order, including an earlier note waiting behind its resident's move. Returning from a hidden tab or a render gap longer than the 30-second cadence discards pending walks, cards, and effects and refreshes from now. These returns add no account of what happened while the picture was absent.
5. **The room log wraps and scrolls.** It starts blank and keeps at most 200 newly witnessed entries across all public rooms, newest at the bottom. It does not backfill the day or keep separate room histories. Changing the followed resident or entering a quiet room clears it. Only activity witnessed in the displayed room is added; those entries remain when the viewer moves to another public room. Text wraps to the available full width and is never cut off. Verified long notes show whole, without ellipses. Until a cut note body has been read and verified, its delivered text ends with the honest suffix “(rest not read)”; the hidden-speaker line uses the same rule. Note reads never delay the first picture. Witnessing writes the log immediately with a finite observation time, even if a visual is held or never starts. Animation cues have their own timing and never append log entries. Each entry keeps its own DOM node so a screen reader hears additions without the whole room being announced again. The strip stays about three lines tall on desktop and two on the phone; it scrolls inside itself without moving the pickers. New entries follow the bottom unless the reader has scrolled back. The page itself never scrolls. Other-room and quiet-room activity stay out. The room name, names, speech cards, action captions, this quiet log, and honest read status remain the only on-screen words.
6. **Controls stay small.** There is one resident picker and one place picker; each choice shows the name and the number (resident #id, place #id). Those are the only controls. The page runs at wall-clock time. A resident marked asleep by the public census has no figure, name label, speech card, seat in the room, or resident-picker choice. Going to sleep immediately releases an existing speech card. If the followed resident falls asleep, stop following, stay in this room, and say why in the log. They appear again when a later census refresh says they are awake. The log reports newly witnessed sleep and wake changes.
7. **Night is soft and warm.** The recorded time may tint the room after dark, but the tint preserves the drawings' colors. Recorded thing-use glow and lit windows remain.
8. **Desktop and phone share the same room.** The canvas uses all available space. Desktop keeps the two pickers in one compact footer row. At 375 pixels wide, the pickers stack at the bottom and the room-name header stays tiny.

## Rules that always hold

- Draw only recorded facts. Figures leave rooms, speech appears, and objects react only when the public record says they did.
- Positions within a room are presentation. PR one introduced standing-band allocation; PR two derives it from the current 56-pixel resident art, with a 60-pixel pitch that leaves room for the bob. Thing art is 32 pixels. The shared allocator reserves each thing's complete 32-pixel footprint and each resident's complete 56-pixel footprint. Things keep stable spots while residents take decoration-only steps. Things and residents have a small rounded cream label with a soft shadow, sized to the text plus padding up to 124 pixels. Long names keep their beginning followed by three dots; hover on a computer or tap on a phone to reveal the full name. A tiny kind mark appears for things when it fits. While an action caption is showing, its resident and used thing labels win label collisions. Other labels keep the collision rule, so hidden labels stay hidden. Names use a fixed 13-pixel screen size and device-density text textures with linear filtering, while drawing textures use nearest-neighbour filtering. Text resolution refreshes when the window moves between displays, including the room name. The canvas backing store follows device density, with a compensated camera zoom and unchanged CSS room size, so pixel drawings stay crisp on dense screens. Resident decorations preserve their original glyph scale as they grow with the resident drawing, including outlined activity cues beside the head. Offsets from carried things grow with the thing art. It hides clipped name plates and plates that collide with other plates or figures, while plates that just touch an edge remain eligible. Speakers take seats before idle figures, including a followed idle figure. If several speakers cannot all fit, the room log names the newest unshown speaker and shows the complete recorded note; quiet-room privacy still applies. Other occupants can be hidden when space runs out, so the picture summarises crowded rooms rather than serving as a census. Unchanged frames reuse their placements, and displaced figures return to their preferred spot when it is free. A recorded walk reserves its route and full-size target. When no safe target or path exists, the figure is not drawn for that walk, while its recorded room membership still updates and the quiet activity line describes the move. The viewer never invents an inside-room teleport. Hidden occupants return as room space allows, with speakers taking priority.
- Quiet rooms show their name and no residents, things, speech, or activity. A short status explains why the room is empty, including when following a resident there.
- All city access is anonymous and read-only. The viewer has no key, wallet, credential, or city write path.
- If a required presence, directory, or change-feed read fails, freeze the last successful picture and retry politely. A missing or failed room outline affects only its things list: keep the last floor contents, mark that outline unmergeable, and show one muted issue line. Read issues belong to the current successful cycle and clear when that cycle has no issues; late failures from an older cycle cannot pin an old message. Do not advance walks, speech, activity, or current placement until a complete refresh succeeds. State the read failure honestly in `#live-status`, then clear it after success. A drawing area too small for the standing band plus one figure has its own status. Witnessed walks finish hidden at their destination while it is too small, freeing live delivery; figures are seated again when the view is enlarged. This presentation status never claims the public record failed. With the current margins and figures, the minimum drawing area is 168 by 242 pixels.
- Exact counts belong in the city's other pages. This view never claims a number it did not read.
- A selected public room follows its direct thing pages up to 200 things and 200 page reads, keeps every returned thing as a room member, and reads each actually shown thing's drawing once unless a supplied flag says it has none; 32-pixel things have their own smaller spacing so 23 fit in an otherwise empty room at 375 by 812 pixels, while fuller or shorter rooms hide overflow and colliding name plates until space opens.

## Four pull requests

1. **Page and live-room skeleton.** Earlier delivery used a replay checkpoint and loaded-day activity at startup. That startup contract is superseded by the current-state and feed-head rules above. The delivered room shell, resident and place pickers, activity line, honest status, standing allocation, collision handling, and shared positions remain.
2. **Art and readable words.** Tile place drawings at 32 pixels with 34% shade so small objects remain clear. Use one warm plain floor for undrawn rooms. Show 56-pixel residents and 32-pixel things, with fixed 13-pixel names and complete 14-pixel speech, with bounded timing that does not block live delivery. Night uses a warm brown/plum tint capped at 14%, with smooth transitions, lit windows and recorded thing glow. Floor, figure, name, bubble-growth and night-tint policies are plain tested functions.
3. **Recorded motion.** Deliver the 140-CSS-pixel-per-second source-to-door departure and door-to-safe-target arrival paths, safe 12–24-pixel idle steps, and room-wide speech queues described above. Moves sharing a doorway wait for the earlier walk; a busy doorway does not discard an arrival. The earlier startup suppression rule is superseded: startup begins at the feed head, witnessed moves walk, and later census relocations snap. Keep motion, allocation, queueing, and timing policies in pure functions with unit tests.
4. **Remove the old viewer.** The page contains only the one-room live viewer and its room log. Recorded scene fixtures and fake-clock replay remain test tooling.

The owner-feedback work after PR three adds idle bobbing, removes sleepers and their seats, lets the full room log wrap and scroll in a fixed strip, changes speech from pages to scrolling cards, and replaces plain name plates with cream labels. Long labels now use three dots with a full-name hover or tap reveal. The current 56-pixel figures, 140-CSS-pixel-per-second safe motion, readable words, and warm cream labels remain too. Its real-city capture outputs are `pr-4-live-desktop.png` and `pr-4-live-phone.png`.

The action-animation follow-up adds four presentation rules:

1. A witnessed use or consume in the shown public room routes its awake actor at 140 CSS pixels per second to a free spot beside the visible thing, shakes both figures and their names for one second, then plays the existing glow or crumbs; the actor stays there and resumes idle movement, while unavailable targets receive only the action caption.
2. Each witnessed resident action except speech gets a cream caption using the log's words without the actor name, stacked clear of the actor's name and speech card, with failed attempts retaining their motif and all captions disappearing on room changes. Most captions last three seconds; a move caption stays through the complete departure and ends at the room boundary.
3. A followed visible resident carries a small steady cream chevron that moves with the figure and disappears when following stops.
4. Successful effect resolutions and moves without a represented walk have distinct small pixel motifs instead of the plain dot.

Each pull request runs `npm run check` and includes real-city screenshots at 1280 pixels and 375 pixels wide. Before each capture, wait for the first change poll and the selected room's outline to resolve (merged or unmergeable after a missing, failed, or hidden-room answer), finish pending picture reads including place drawings, and hold a stable displayed things count for one second. Quiet ancestors use the same visibility rule for drawing and settlement, and a hidden room does not wait for an outline read that cannot start. Every capture sample is bounded by the remaining timeout. New pure behavior gets unit coverage. Browser checks cover only the small user flows and prove that growing a small window redraws figures. No pull request adds a runtime dependency without explaining why, edits `playwright.config.ts` to change browsers, or commits `dist/`.
