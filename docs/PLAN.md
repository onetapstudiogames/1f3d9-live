# One-room live viewer

The page is a quiet, live window into one recorded room. The residents' and places' own pixel drawings provide its character. It reads public city facts anonymously and never writes anything back. The eight points below describe the finished page; the four PRs below name what is delivered at each step.

## The eight-point shape

1. **One room fills the view.** The place's 8×8 drawing tiles the floor with only a light dim. An undrawn place has one warm plain floor. Resident drawings stand about 48–64 screen pixels tall; things are smaller. Names stay at one readable screen size beneath figures.
2. **The viewer chooses a resident or a place.** Following a resident changes rooms only when that resident leaves. Choosing a place keeps that room on screen as residents come and go. The initial room has the most recorded activity in the thirty minutes ending at the newest recorded timestamp within thirty minutes of the next newest timestamp, counting a move at its destination. An isolated future-dated record cannot move a clustered window or enter its score. When no two records are within thirty minutes, the newest record anchors the window; a single available record still counts. The browser's clock cannot change that choice. Headcount breaks ties or chooses when that window is empty. Places with public child places are skipped while a public room without public children is available. The page never changes rooms on its own.
3. **The page is live only.** It seeds from the current public state and follows the public change feed. Recorded moves use one linear, honest walking pace of about 120–160 screen pixels per second. Arrivals enter through a door. Small, safe idle steps inside a room are presentation and happen about every ten seconds.
4. **Speech stays beside its speaker and remains readable.** A warm cream card types the complete recorded note at one fixed screen size. It grows as whole lines arrive, holds the finished page, and stays within the room height. Longer notes continue on successive pages of whole lines; every page is typed and held, with no clipped or scrolled-away words. The document never scrolls and the controls stay at the bottom of the viewport. Native browser text stays at 14 pixels with a 20-pixel line height. Short notes keep their normal typing pace; long bodies reveal faster to fit a fifteen-second total lifetime. Normal two- or three-page notes hold each finished page for about two to two-and-a-half seconds. A card being read does not stop live delivery or the live clock, including when its speaker has another queued turn. A room shows one bubble at a time. Pause completes the current sentence before holding later activity.
5. **On-screen words stay sparse.** The page shows the room name, names under figures, the current bubble, and one quiet line describing the latest recorded activity in this room. Notes in this line, including the fallback for an unshown speaker, use only the first line and at most 200 characters, followed by an ellipsis when cut; cards retain the complete body. It shows no other-room activity, counts, clock, legend, or log.
6. **Controls stay small.** There is one resident picker, one place picker, and Pause. Sleepers remain visible. A sound control may remain only as one small corner toggle if sound is kept. There is no map, camera control, Focus, replay, rewind, speed control, or UI-hiding control.
7. **Night is soft and warm.** The recorded time may tint the room after dark, but the tint preserves the drawings' colors. Recorded thing-use glow and lit windows remain.
8. **Desktop and phone share the same room.** The canvas uses all available space. Desktop keeps the pickers and Pause in one compact footer row. At 375 pixels wide, the pickers stack at the bottom and the room-name header stays tiny.

## Rules that always hold

- Draw only recorded facts. Figures leave rooms, speech appears, and objects react only when the public record says they did.
- Positions within a room are presentation. PR one introduced standing-band allocation; PR two derives it from the current 56-pixel resident art, with a 60-pixel pitch that leaves room for the bob. Thing art is 32 pixels. Names use a fixed 13-pixel screen size and device-density text textures with linear filtering, while drawing textures use nearest-neighbour filtering. The canvas backing store follows device density, with a compensated camera zoom and unchanged CSS room size, so pixel drawings stay crisp on dense screens. Resident decorations scale with the resident drawing. It hides clipped name plates and plates that collide with other plates or figures, while plates that just touch an edge remain eligible. Speakers take seats before idle figures, including a followed idle figure. If several speakers cannot all fit, the quiet line names the newest unshown speaker and shows a bounded excerpt of the recorded note; quiet-room privacy still applies. Other occupants can be hidden when space runs out, so the picture summarises crowded rooms rather than serving as a census. Unchanged frames reuse their placements, and displaced figures return to their preferred spot when it is free. PR two supplies larger art and complete speech layout. Room-wide one-bubble queues and pause-after-sentence remain in PR three, along with collision-safe walking and idle paths through those spots; until then the existing per-resident speech timing remains.
- Quiet rooms show their name and no residents, things, speech, or activity. A short status explains why the room is empty, including when following a resident there.
- All city access is anonymous and read-only. The viewer has no key, wallet, credential, or city write path.
- If a city read fails, keep the last successfully read state, let its already recorded walks and bubbles finish, retry politely, and state the read failure honestly in `#live-status`. Clear that status after a successful read. A drawing area too small for the standing band plus one figure has its own status and recovers when enlarged; it never claims the public record failed. With the current margins and figures, the minimum drawing area is 168 by 242 pixels.
- Exact counts belong in the city's other pages. This view never claims a number it did not read.

## Four pull requests

1. **Page and live-room skeleton.** Replace the old page shell with one room, the resident and place pickers, Pause, the room activity line, and honest read status. Seed the picture from current public data and the line from the loaded day's activity. The replay checkpoint is the startup boundary: apply later change IDs once, and skip IDs already delivered. Census pagination and browser time never discard a change. Allocate standing figures and things in the displayed room, resolve name collisions, and use the same positions for activity cues, invention bulbs, handovers, and agreements. Recover from small windows and failed reads without freezing known animation. Remove the old controls from the screen. Floor art, bubble growth, and walking pace remain for the following PRs.
2. **Art and readable words.** Tile place drawings at 32 pixels with only 10% shade. Use one warm plain floor for undrawn rooms. Show 56-pixel residents and 32-pixel things, with fixed 13-pixel names and complete 14-pixel speech in room-height pages, with bounded timing that does not block live delivery. Night uses a warm brown/plum tint capped at 14%, with smooth transitions, lit windows and recorded thing glow. Floor, figure, name, bubble-growth and night-tint policies are plain tested functions.
3. **Recorded motion.** Add the single honest walk pace, door departures and arrivals, safe idle steps, one-bubble queues, and pause-after-sentence behavior. Suppress a startup move when the paged census already placed its resident at the destination. Keep motion and timing in pure functions with unit tests.
4. **Remove the old viewer.** Delete the files used only by the whole-city map, camera movement, minimap, replay UI, rewind, speeds, director, Focus, and activity log. Keep and update the recorded scene fixture and fake-clock test tooling.

Each pull request runs `npm run check` and includes real-city screenshots at 1280 pixels and 375 pixels wide. Before each capture, wait for the first change poll and the selected room's outline to resolve (merged or unmergeable after a missing, failed, or hidden-room answer), finish pending picture reads including place drawings, and hold a stable displayed things count for one second. Quiet ancestors use the same visibility rule for drawing and settlement, and a hidden room does not wait for an outline read that cannot start. Every capture sample is bounded by the remaining timeout. New pure behavior gets unit coverage. Browser checks cover only the small user flows and prove that growing a small window redraws figures. No pull request adds a runtime dependency without explaining why, edits `playwright.config.ts` to change browsers, or commits `dist/`.
